# Anchoring — Ground Tackle and Singlehanded Procedure

The CD25D has no windlass. Every anchoring decision — anchor weight, rode length, scope — has to account for manual retrieval. The boat's heavy displacement (5,120 lbs, D/L 333.24) means it exerts higher loads than a light 25-footer of similar length, which affects anchor and chain sizing.

---

## Primary Anchor — Modern Scoop Type

**Recommended:** Rocna, Mantus, or Spade — approximately **22 lbs**.

These second-generation modern anchors (scoop/roll-bar designs) set fast, reset reliably after wind/tide shifts, and hold well in Penobscot Bay's dominant bottom types: soft mud, sand, and mixed. They outperform traditional plough anchors (CQR/Delta) in independent testing across most conditions.

| Anchor | Weight | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Rocna 10** | 22 lbs | Roll-bar sets on contact; galvanized or stainless; strong performance across bottom types |
| **Mantus M1 25** | 25 lbs | Excellent mud and sand performance; disassembles for stowage; US-made |
| **Spade S80** | ~22 lbs | Hollow shank; arguably best raw holding power per pound; no roll bar (shank acts as orienter) |

**Sizing note:** 22 lbs is appropriate for the CD25D's displacement. Manufacturer sizing charts often assume average displacement for a given LOA — the CD25D's heavy displacement warrants sizing up from "25-foot" recommendations.

### Why Not a Traditional Plough

The existing plough (CQR pattern or Delta-type, if aboard) is not worthless — it has one advantage: it handles rocky or weedy bottoms where scoop anchors can clog. Keep it as backup. But for primary use, a modern scoop anchor sets faster and resets more reliably after a 180° shift.

## Secondary Anchor — Fortress Aluminum

**Recommended secondary: Fortress FX-11 (7 lbs minimum).** Do not use FX-7.

The Fortress is a high-performance aluminum Danforth pattern. Exceptional holding power-to-weight ratio in sand and soft mud. Indispensable as:
- Lightweight kedge anchor (deployable from the cockpit in an emergency)
- Stern anchor in a Med-moor situation
- Backup when primary is deployed and you need a second hook

### FX-7 vs. FX-11 Sizing

| Model | Weight | Rated Holding (sand) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| FX-7 | 4 lbs | 1,025 lbs | **Too small for CD25D.** Appropriate as stern anchor only or for daysail-only use in calm conditions. |
| **FX-11** | 7 lbs | 2,375 lbs | **Minimum for CD25D.** Appropriate as secondary/kedge. Light enough to handle solo. |
| FX-16 | 11 lbs | 3,375 lbs | More margin; still light; appropriate if budget allows |

Cape Dory owners on the CD25, CD28, and CD30 consistently report upsizing significantly from manufacturer recommendations — the heavy displacement class is demanding on anchor systems.

### Fortress Critical Weakness

**The Fortress does not reset reliably after a 180° wind/tide shift.**

After a shift, the Fortress can break out and lie flat on the bottom without re-anchoring. This is a known failure mode documented in independent testing. In Penobscot Bay, overnight conditions with tidal reversals make this a real concern.

**Mitigation:** Use the Fortress as secondary, not primary, when tidal reversals are expected. Use a modern scoop (Rocna/Mantus/Spade) as primary — these reset reliably after shifts.

## Rode — Combination Chain + Nylon

**Recommended setup:**

- **25–30 ft of 1/4" G4 (High Test) chain** — provides catenary weight at the anchor end, abrasion protection at the shackle, and reduces snubbing in chop
- **150–200 ft of 1/2" three-strand nylon** — provides elasticity to absorb surge loads; three-strand is preferred for spliceability
- **Rope-to-chain splice** — cleaner than a shackle, lies flat on the bow roller, no metal hardware to chafe or fail

> **Note:** 1/4" BBB (proof coil) chain is sometimes substituted — it's compatible with windlass wildcat fittings on boats with windlasses, but the CD25D has no windlass. G4 (High Test) is stronger for the same weight and is the better choice for a manual setup.

### Stowage Without a Windlass

Because the CD25D has no anchor locker with a proper hawse pipe, the rode lives in the forepeak or on deck. Four-point pre-departure checklist:

1. **Anchor seated** in the bow roller or stowed on the foredeck
2. **Rode cleated** — bitter end secured to a structural point (not just an ear cleat)
3. **Retaining pin** through the shackle or anchor eye
4. **Safety lashing** — one wrap of line to prevent the anchor bouncing off the bow roller underway

## Singlehanded Anchoring Procedure

### Choosing a Spot

- Read current and forecast wind — anchor in the lee of the expected wind, not current wind
- Note swing room: the CD25D on 7:1 scope in 10 ft of water swings in a circle ~70 ft radius; check for nearby boats, shoals, and hazards at the edge of the swing arc
- Identify bottom type (chart, local knowledge) — soft mud and sand are the CD25D's targets; avoid rocky bottom without a plough
- In Penobscot Bay: sticky mud in Rockland and most protected anchorages; sand in Fox Islands Thorofare and exposed bays

### Scope

| Conditions | Minimum Scope |
|---|---|
| Calm, short stay | 5:1 |
| Overnight, moderate wind | 7:1 |
| Overnight, strong wind | 10:1 |

Scope ratio = rode out / depth of water. Always measure from bow roller height, not waterline.

**Practical calculation:** In 10 ft of water with 4 ft of freeboard at the bow roller = 14 ft effective depth. 7:1 scope = 98 ft of rode. Use round numbers — deploy 100 ft.

### Dropping the Anchor (Solo)

1. Motor slowly upwind or upcurrent toward the target spot
2. Stop the boat; let momentum die completely
3. Lower the anchor hand-over-hand — do not throw it; dropping cleanly without tangling the chain is the priority
4. As the boat drifts back, pay out rode steadily to match drift
5. When scope is out: **snub the rode on the bow cleat** to load the anchor
6. **Power-set:** motor astern at ~1,000 RPM for 30–60 seconds — watch the bow for movement; a good set feels solid, a dragging anchor lurches
7. Take visual bearings on two fixed objects ashore (buildings, lights, trees); note GPS position
8. Set an anchor alarm on the chartplotter

### Confirming the Set

A set anchor holds against engine power without the bow moving. Two failure signs:
- Bow walks sideways under power astern — dragging
- Rode goes slack and re-tightens rhythmically — anchor not set, bouncing

If dragging: motor forward to relieve load, haul anchor, find a better spot or let out more scope.

### Anchor Watch Singlehanded

On overnight passages, a GPS anchor alarm (on chartplotter or app) is the primary watch-keeping tool. Set the alarm radius conservatively: half the swing radius. **Pulpit Harbor and other popular Penobscot Bay anchorages can be crowded July–August** — set a tight alarm and check periodically, especially on wind shifts.

## Weighing Anchor (No Windlass)

Manual retrieval on the CD25D:

1. **Motor slowly toward the anchor** — this is the most important step; do not try to haul rode against a loaded anchor
2. As you motor up, pull the rode hand-over-hand and coil or flake it on deck
3. When the rode goes **vertical** (straight up and down from bow), cleat it off
4. Let the **boat's momentum and buoyancy break the anchor free** — motor forward slightly, or wait for a small wave lift
5. Once broken out, haul the anchor up quickly; wash mud off before it comes aboard
6. **Secure immediately** before motoring — seat the anchor in the roller, set the retaining pin, safety-lash

> **Singlehanding note:** The most dangerous moment is when the anchor breaks out and you are alone at the bow with the helm unsecured. Either: (a) put the engine in neutral and let the boat drift while you retrieve, or (b) set the tiller pilot to hold course before going forward. Never leave the helm unattended with the engine in gear.

## Winch Retrieval System (Chain Load Management)

When the chain load exceeds what you can hand-haul (deep anchorage, stuck anchor, adverse current):

**The setup:** chain hook → 1/2" line → snatch block on foredeck cleat → cockpit genoa winch

1. Attach a **chain hook** (see below) to a chain link with a 1/2" retrieval line
2. Lead the line through a **snatch block** shackled to a bow cleat or deck eye
3. Run the line back to the **cockpit genoa winch**
4. Grind the winch; the chain hook pulls the chain forward; when the line is fully in, cleat the chain at the bow, reset the hook further back on the chain, repeat

**Critical safety rules:**
- **Never let anchor chain contact the winch drum** — chain will damage or destroy the winch and can jam catastrophically
- Only the retrieval line goes on the winch; chain stays on the deck and bow cleat
- The snatch block must be secured to a structural point — use a bow cleat with a backing plate, not a toe rail or genoa track

> **Balsa-core deck warning:** The CD25D has a balsa-cored deck. Chafe from chain on deck can compromise the core if the deck covering is breached. Use a chafe mat or lead chain over a rubber pad at the bow. Inspect any areas where chain habitually rests against the deck.

### Chain Hook Selection

| Type | Example | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Grab hook (hook only) | Generic 1/4" grab hook | Cheapest; can release under shock load |
| **Plate hook (chain grip)** | **Wichard Chain Grip** | Stainless; squeezes chain as load increases; preferred |
| Locking hook | Mantus Chain Hook | Locking gate prevents release; good for snubbers |

Match hook to chain: **1/4" chain = 1/4" hook.** Do not mix sizes.

**Best option for the retrieval system:** Wichard Chain Grip or equivalent plate-style hook — loads more reliably under shock than a simple grab hook and doesn't require a locking gate to be safe in tension.

**For snubbers:** Mantus Chain Hook with locking gate is appropriate — the locking feature prevents the snubber from releasing on a slack rode.

### Snatch Block Selection

For the CD25D anchor retrieval rig, the snatch block must handle the combined load of chain + anchor in adverse current. **Minimum SWL: 1,500 lbs.**

| Brand/Model | Notes |
|---|---|
| Harken 75mm Snatch Block | Heavy-duty, simple, reliable; premium price |
| Lewmar Synchro Snatch Block | Low-friction Synchro design; 60mm model for this use |
| Ronstan Series 60 Utility Snatch Block | Good balance of performance and value; popular among cruisers |
| **Garhauer 25-SB** | All-stainless; "practically indestructible"; best price-to-strength ratio; order direct from Garhauer |

**Used hardware:** The Marine Exchange in Kittery, ME (just across the bridge from Portsmouth, NH) stocks quality used marine hardware — worth checking for a snatch block at a fraction of new price.

## Anchor Rode Depth Marking

Mark the chain rode at 25-foot intervals so you can read scope without counting coils. Zip ties threaded through chain links are the most durable, low-cost solution.

**Color code (25-foot intervals):**

| Mark | Depth | Color |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 25 ft | Blue |
| 2 | 50 ft | Green |
| 3 | 75 ft | Red |
| 4 | 100 ft | White |
| 5 | 125 ft | Yellow |

**Installation:**
1. Thread ties **through chain links** — not around the outside. Ties threaded through can't rotate or slide off; external ties work loose.
2. Use **2–3 ties per mark** — redundancy means a broken tie doesn't eliminate the mark entirely
3. **Trim tails flush** with the head of the tie — protruding tails catch on the bow roller and chafe through
4. At the **last 15–20 feet of rode before the bitter end**, install a **warning cluster of 4–5 red ties** — this is the "stop paying out" signal

> **Penobscot Bay note:** In 10–12 ft tidal range anchorages, knowing your deployed scope accurately matters. High water can put you in 17 ft; low water in 5 ft — same chain, very different scope ratios. Accurate marking lets you adjust quickly.

## Snubber

A **chain snubber** absorbs surge loads on the chain rode. With only 25–30 ft of chain and a long nylon rode, the nylon's elasticity handles most surge. But in a crowded anchorage or with a short rode, add a snubber:

1. Attach a chain hook to the chain ~2–3 ft forward of the bow roller
2. Run a 10–15 ft piece of 1/2" three-strand nylon from the hook to the cleat
3. Let out a foot of rode so the load transfers to the nylon snubber — the chain hangs slack

This dramatically reduces snubbing loads and noise. Available as complete assemblies from West Marine or rigging shops.

## Penobscot Bay — Anchoring Notes

- **Bottom:** Predominantly soft mud in protected anchorages (Pulpit Harbor, Perry Creek, most of Fox Islands Thorofare). Mud is excellent for modern scoop anchors and forgiving of imprecise scope. Rockland Harbor bottom is sticky mud — good holding but retrieval is messy.
- **Tides:** 10–12 ft range at Rockland; up to 18 ft in upper bay. Swing room calculations must account for tide-driven depth changes. At low tide in 5 ft, 7:1 scope = 35 ft of rode. At high tide in 17 ft, same ground requires 119 ft.
- **Lobster pots:** Anchoring in lobster pot fields puts warp under the boat at risk of fouling the prop on departure. Enter unfamiliar anchorages slowly and check visually for pots.
- **Tidal reversals:** Overnight anchoring in the bay means tidal reversals. A primary scoop anchor (Rocna/Mantus/Spade) handles this; the Fortress secondary does not.
- **Wind shifts:** August thunderstorms can bring rapid 180° wind shifts. Power-set at arrival so the anchor is firmly bedded before conditions change.
