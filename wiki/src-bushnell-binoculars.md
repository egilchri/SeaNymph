## Summary

Two AI research responses covering the Bushnell Marine 7x50 binoculars — one on features/waterproofing, one on warranty.

The Bushnell Marine 7x50 is a purpose-built marine binocular: 100% waterproof (hermetically sealed, nitrogen-purged), saltwater-resistant exterior, and notably **buoyant** — it will float if dropped overboard. It includes an illuminated internal compass with a ranging reticle and uses individual eyepiece focus (each eye focused separately) rather than a center-focus ring. BaK-4 prisms, 50mm objectives. Priced around $305.89 (vs. Steiner Navigator 7x50c at ~$690).

The H2O 7x50 is a cheaper Bushnell option (~$96.14) that is also waterproof but lacks a compass — incorrect choice if navigation bearing capability is required.

On warranty: Bushnell offers the "Ironclad Warranty." For binoculars manufactured after June 2020, this is a 20-year warranty covering defects in materials and workmanship. It is fully transferable, requires no receipt or registration, and follows the product — not the original owner. The exception: **electronic components** (illuminated compass) are covered for only **5 years**. Claims go through bushnell.com/pages/warranty; the owner pays inbound shipping, Bushnell pays return shipping.

## Key Takeaways

- Bushnell Marine 7x50 is 100% waterproof, nitrogen-purged, and **buoyant** (floats if dropped)
- Illuminated compass with ranging reticle built in
- Uses **individual eyepiece focus**, not center focus — sharper per eye but slower to re-adjust
- No Sports-Auto-Focus equivalent — requires manual focus adjustment when conditions change
- Priced ~$305.89; roughly half the cost of Steiner Navigator 7x50c
- H2O 7x50 (~$96.14) is waterproof but has no compass — not appropriate for navigation use
- Bushnell Ironclad Warranty: 20-year structural/optical (for post-June-2020 units), fully transferable
- Electronics (illuminated compass) warranty is 5 years only
- No receipt required to make a warranty claim

## Contradictions / Surprises

- The individual eyepiece focus system is often described as "preferred for rolling seas" because each eye is optimally focused. However, it is also slower to use than center focus and has no equivalent to Steiner's Sports-Auto-Focus (set once, sharp from 20m to ∞). For singlehanding where one-handed, rapid use is critical, this is a notable tradeoff.
- Bushnell electronics warranty (5 years) is actually longer than Steiner's (3 years for compass illumination circuit) — a minor point in Bushnell's favor.
- The floating feature is a meaningful safety advantage that the Steiner Navigator does not share — losing $690 binoculars overboard is worse than losing $306 ones.
