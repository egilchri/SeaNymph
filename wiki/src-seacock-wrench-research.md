## Summary

Detailed analysis of tools for through-hull removal on a classic vessel. Covers step wrenches (Buck Algonquin vs. Groco), their limitations on old Spartan fittings, economical alternatives (radiator spud wrench, internal pipe wrench), DIY modified socket technique, and the case for embracing destructive removal (angle grinder) as the standard professional approach rather than a last resort.

## Key Takeaways

- Buck Algonquin step wrench (~$50–75) may not fit original Spartan through-hulls — documented incompatibility
- Groco THT-530 (~$150–185) is marketed as an installation tool; cast aluminum may break under removal torque
- Radiator spud wrench from Home Depot (~$25) is functionally equivalent to marine step wrench
- Internal pipe wrench (nipple extractor) grips full inner circumference — mechanically superior for removal
- DIY modified socket: grind two slots to match through-hull lugs; works with breaker bar
- Angle grinder destructive removal is what professional yards routinely do first — not a failure
