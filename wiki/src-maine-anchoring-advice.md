# Anchoring Advice for Maine — CCA / Tom Babbitt

**Author:** Tom Babbitt
**Origin:** Cruising Club of America (CCA) online cruising guide
**File:** raw/MaineAnchoringAdvice.md

## Summary

A practical guide to anchoring in Maine from an experienced cruiser. Covers general anchoring wisdom applicable anywhere, then Maine-specific considerations. Particularly useful for the rope vs. chain rode debate — the author has extensive experience with both and gives an honest accounting of the failure modes of each.

## Key Takeaways

- **Circle the drop site first** — two slow decreasing-radius circles before anchoring verify actual vs. charted depth and confirm swing room; charts can be wrong in either direction
- **"If it looks too good to be true, it probably is"** — an empty spot in a crowded anchorage usually has bad holding (rock, hardpan, kelp)
- **Trust your gut** — if a spot doesn't feel right, move on
- **Rode markers are a safety issue** — author dragged in the Caribbean because markers had worn away; had unknowingly reduced scope to 1.5:1
- **Rope rode can unlay** — a 6-month-old top-of-line three-strand rope completely unlaid after a 30-knot Caribbean night; inspect nylon rode for twist and strand separation
- **"Welding the pile"** — all-chain rode can settle into a solid immovable lump after heavy offshore use in 20–30 knots; a real failure mode
- **Keel wrapping** — rope+chain rode can wrap around CD25D's full keel in tidal anchorages with current reversals; all-chain minimizes this risk
- **Lobster gear at anchor** — as boat moves up/down with tide and swings with wind/current, rode or hull can entangle in lobster warp; pot buoy can bounce off hull; locate gear before getting underway in morning
- **Afternoon sea breeze** — delay departure to catch the post-lunch filling; but then arrive late in popular anchorages — a real Maine planning tension
- Maine bottom is primarily mud — excellent holding, but sticky cleanup; wash-down pump invaluable
- Recommended reading: *Happy Hooking: The Art of Anchoring* (Blackwell), *Attainable Adventure Cruising* (Harries/Nickel)

## Rode Debate Summary (from Article)

**40 ft chain + rope:**
- Pros: easier handling without windlass, less mud on rope, better sailing performance (less bow weight)
- Cons: less catenary, chafe risk, keel/rudder entanglement in shifts, longer scope requirements, windlass struggles with rope-to-chain transition as rode ages, rope can unlay

**All-chain (or 3-4x normal anchoring depth):**
- Pros: shorter scope (more flexibility in crowded areas), no chafe, no rope-to-chain transition, peace of mind in blow
- Cons: muddy cleanup, bow weight, windlass required (cost/complexity/weight), "welding the pile" in heavy offshore use

## Contradictions / Surprises

> The rope unlaying failure is a warning not previously in the wiki. The author explicitly calls it a "top of the line" rode that was only 6 months old — this is not a cheap-rope problem. All three-strand nylon is susceptible under sustained high load.

> The "welding the pile" failure mode for all-chain is a counterpoint to the common assumption that all-chain is strictly superior. In an offshore passage context, chain can become impossible to deploy.

> No contradictions with existing wiki anchoring content — this article reinforces and enriches it.
