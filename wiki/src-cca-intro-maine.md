# Introduction to Cruising Maine — CCA / Bob Rubadeau

**Author:** R. J. Rubadeau
**Origin:** Cruising Club of America (CCA) online cruising guide

## Summary

A narrative and practical introduction to cruising the Maine coast by a sailor with nearly fifty years of experience in these waters. Written for the CCA's cruising guide site (cruisingclub.org) as a complement to the Taft Guide. Covers the full coast from Kittery/Pepperell Cove to Roque Island near the Bay of Fundy.

The tone is personal and vivid, but the practical content is solid — particularly the lobster pot toggle anatomy, the tidal current magnitude, and the spring tide warning. The article explicitly recommends the Taft Guide (Hank & Jan Taft and Curtis Rindlaub) as the comprehensive reference, with this article providing "timely anecdotal" supplementation.

## Key Takeaways

- **Fog:** Doesn't care — use radar, AIS, chartplotter, paper charts, compass; learn your instruments in clear weather; a Mainer's warning about morning fog is not casual ("the fog could be a might thick come mornin'")
- **Tidal currents:** Can run to 4 knots in narrow channels; 1 hour of beam current puts destination 1 mile off course; strong winds opposing current create steep chop
- **Spring (minus) tides:** Water falls below chart datum; spots that show 2 feet of clearance on the chart can go dry
- **Granite ledge hazard:** Depth sounder reads 30 feet while bow is near rock — steep granite shelves are not gradual; chart + slow speed + eyes beat depth sounder near ledges
- **Lobster pot toggle anatomy:** Two floats per trap — main banded float + smaller "toggle" float 10–15 ft up the warp. The loop of warp between them hangs just below surface = what fouls props. Seeing the main float is not enough — watch for the toggle and its relationship to the main float.
- **Leave early, anchor by noon** — SW breeze builds through the day; morning passages are calmer; early arrival gives anchorage options
- **Focus on one area per trip** — Maine is vast; tight schedules fail against weather and tide; one area thoroughly explored beats a sprint up the coast
- **August-September** are the most dependable sailing months (least fog, most settled)
- **Roque Island** is the traditional "Holy Grail" for Maine cruisers — far Downeast; serious navigation required
- **Jericho Bay** specifically named for lobster pot hazards (tidal currents stream warps horizontally)
- **Muscle Ridge Channel** described as "always-a-handful" — use caution on approach to Penobscot Bay from south
- **MITA** (Maine Island Trail Association) — 255 sites, mita.org/join
- **MCHT** (Maine Coast Heritage Trust) — 120+ free public preserves, mcht.org/visit-a-preserve

## Cruising Route (Full Maine Summary)

Pepperell Cove/Kittery → Biddeford Pool → Casco Bay/Portland/Falmouth → Cape Small → Kennebec River/Sheepscot River/Boothbay Harbor → Muscongus Bay → Port Clyde → Tenants Harbor → Muscle Ridge Channel → Owls Head → **Penobscot Bay** (Rockland, Camden, Fox Islands Thorofare, Vinalhaven, North Haven, Islesboro, Castine, Belfast) → Jericho Bay → Deer Isle Thorofare/Stonington → Merchant Row → Blue Hill Bay → Mount Desert Island (SW Harbor, NE Harbor, Seal Harbor, Bar Harbor, Somes Sound, Acadia) → Frenchman's Bay → Schoodic Point → Downeast (Mud Hole, Great Wass, Jonesport, Cutler, Roque Island)
