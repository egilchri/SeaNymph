# Mooring Advice and Etiquette in Maine — CCA / Tom Babbitt

**Author:** Tom Babbitt
**Origin:** Cruising Club of America (CCA) online cruising guide
**File:** raw/MooringAdviceForMaine.md

## Summary

A practical guide to evaluating, finding, and using moorings in Maine. Covers the four evaluation criteria (location, condition, size, maintenance), the three types of Maine moorings (rental, guest, private), pickup procedure including hand signals, and the etiquette around private moorings. Written from the perspective of an experienced Penobscot Bay cruiser.

Short article with high information density. The most important finding is experiential: mooring failures have caused more damage than anchoring failures for this experienced sailor. Camden Harbor is specifically noted as requiring annual inspections (with inspection tags on pennants); most other Penobscot Bay harbors have no inspection requirements at all.

## Key Takeaways

- **Mooring failures cause more damage than anchoring mishaps** — evaluate moorings critically, don't assume they're safe
- **Camden Harbor** requires annual inspections + inspection tag on pennant — meaningful assurance; **other Penobscot Bay harbors** have no inspection requirements
- Clean pennant ≠ well-maintained mooring; a 12-year-uninspected mooring still had a clean pennant
- Always give the mooring a tug in reverse before settling in — loads it and stretches bottom chain to reveal actual swing room
- Short pennant can allow anchor to saw through the pennant — result: cast adrift
- Old fiberglass pickup wands cause fiberglass slivers — wear gloves
- Slimy pennant: loop a dock line through the eye instead of handling the pennant directly
- Hand signals: direction, distance, speed from bow to helm; also signal to indicate dropped mooring location so helm doesn't run over it
- Coasting rule: ~1 boat length per knot of speed when rounding up under sail
- Private mooring: if owner arrives you must leave immediately; never leave boat unattended without explicit permission; never use a fisherman's mooring
- Local boat = best source of mooring advice in unfamiliar harbor

## Contradictions / Surprises

> The direct statement that mooring failures have caused more damage than anchoring failures challenges the common assumption that moorings are the "safe" choice. The wiki previously treated anchoring as the primary risk management concern. Both deserve equal scrutiny.

> The Camden Harbor inspection requirement is useful specific knowledge — it means picking up a mooring in Camden with an inspection tag is meaningfully safer than an untagged mooring elsewhere in the bay.
