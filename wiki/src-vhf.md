# Tips for VHF Radio vs Cell Phone — CCA / Brian Guck

**Author:** Brian Guck
**Origin:** Cruising Club of America (CCA) online cruising guide
**File:** raw/VHFRadioVsCellPhone.md

## Summary

A practical overview of communication options for Maine coastal cruising. The article argues that VHF remains essential because cell coverage degrades downeast and offshore, and DSC VHF with MMSI/GPS gives near-shore emergency capability that cell cannot match. Covers Starlink Mini as the modern solution for data connectivity.

Key non-obvious facts: USCG Sector Northern New England has a direct cell number (206-815-7220, non-intuitive area code); foghorns within half a mile can be activated on demand via VHF channel 83A; DSC Distress button triggers the Rescue 21 system which auto-relays to USCG shore stations and self-continues until USCG responds.

## Key Takeaways

- **Cell coverage in Maine:** Decent close to coast; degrades offshore and downeast — do not depend on it for emergencies
- **USCG cell number:** 206-815-7220 (Sector Northern New England; numeric options for Station Rockland; 206 area code is correct)
- **VHF advantage 1:** All vessels within range hear the call — faster local response than USCG
- **VHF advantage 2:** USCG gets approximate bearing within 3 seconds of a ch. 16 transmission; still give lat/lon explicitly
- **DSC requires:** MMSI number + GPS connection — useless without both
- **MMSI:** BoatUS ($25) or FCC ($100 + Ship Station callsign for international)
- **Rescue 21:** DSC Distress button → near-shore quasi-EPIRB; signal relays to USCG shore station; auto-continues until USCG responds on ch. 16
- **Foghorn activation:** Ch. 83A, key mic 5 times → activates fog signals within half-mile on demand
- **Starlink Mini:** ~$229–350 hardware; 12V-to-30V converter required; plans $50–165/month; replaces cell for data but not VHF for safety
- **Group Call:** DSC function useful for club cruises or ad hoc cruiser groups
- **CCA DSC articles:** Charles Starke, free at cruisingclub.org/communications/dsc-groups

## Contradictions / Surprises

> The foghorn activation tip (ch. 83A, 5 mic keys) is completely new to the wiki — not covered anywhere. Very useful in fog.

> The 206 area code for USCG Sector Northern New England is genuinely non-intuitive (206 is Seattle) — the article specifically flags it as "NOT a typo." Worth noting in the wiki prominently.

> No contradictions with existing wiki content. The AIS page covers AIS transponders as a visibility tool; VHF/DSC is a separate and complementary system.
