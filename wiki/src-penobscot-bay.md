## Summary

Comprehensive research on Penobscot Bay sailing conditions, Rockland Harbor mooring options, and digital navigation tools. Key topics: summer fog (advection fog, July-August peak), tidal range (8-18 feet), prevailing SW winds, lobster pot hazards (especially in Jericho Bay where perpendicular tidal currents make warp hard to spot), Pulpit Harbor (North Haven) and Perry Creek (Vinalhaven) as first-night anchorages, Rockland mooring field comparison (Knight Marine vs. Apprenticeshop vs. Rockland Yacht Club vs. Sail Power and Steam Museum vs. Ocean Pursuits), swell analysis (predominantly south/southwest), sticky mud bottom providing good holding. Android navigation apps compared (OpenCPN vs. Navionics vs. C-MAP); OpenCPN is the only app with internal compass deviation support.

## Key Takeaways

- NOAA Chart 13302 is the primary chart for Penobscot Bay and approaches
- "Taft Guide" (A Cruising Guide to the Maine Coast) is the authoritative cruising reference
- Fog is the dominant weather hazard — radar is near-mandatory for Penobscot Bay singlehanding
- Tidal range up to 18 ft; tidal planning essential for multi-day cruises (Casco Passage current significant)
- Lobster pot warps in Jericho Bay are especially hazardous — perpendicular tidal stream makes them horizontal
- Pulpit Harbor, North Haven: best first overnight stop Rockland → SW Harbor; excellent protection all directions
- Rockland Harbor: sticky mud bottom (good holding), south/SW swell dominant, ferry wakes significant near terminal
- Ocean Pursuits (behind breakwater) = best swell protection; Knight Marine = best logistical convenience
- Knight Marine 2026 seasonal rate: $1,850 (includes parking, showers, dinghy dock); Apprenticeshop: $2,050-$2,775
- OpenCPN (paid Android, Dave Register) is the recommended nav app; Nautical Calculator app for offline TVMDC math
- Penobscot Bay max depth 205 ft; deep canyon SE of Rockland (ancient Penobscot River channel) >500 ft
- Hamilton Marine is directly across the street from Knight Marine on Main St — primary local chandlery
