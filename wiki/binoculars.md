# Binoculars

Marine binoculars for SeaNymph: selection criteria, recommended model, and singlehanding considerations.

## Why 7x50

The **7x50** configuration is the universal marine standard. The numbers mean 7× magnification and 50mm objective lens diameter.

- **7× magnification**: High enough to resolve buoys, vessel names, and shoreline features; low enough that hand-shake on a moving boat doesn't make the image useless. Binoculars above 10× become nearly unusable on a pitching deck.
- **50mm objective**: Produces a 7.14mm exit pupil — large enough to fill a dilated human pupil in low-light conditions. This matters at dawn, dusk, and in fog when visibility windows are narrow.
- **Twilight factor of 18.71**: Quantifies low-light performance; the 7x50 significantly outperforms 7x35 or 8x42 in civil twilight.

### Sports-Auto-Focus™

Set the diopters to your eyes once. From that point, everything from 20 meters to the horizon is sharp — no focus ring to fiddle with. **This is critical for singlehanding.** On a pitching deck, alone, trying to locate and maintain sight of a channel buoy while also steering, the last thing you can afford is hunting for focus.

### Compass-in-View

The integrated compass reads in the lower field of the right ocular. You can take a bearing on a landmark, buoy, or vessel **without lowering the binoculars**. Invaluable for fog navigation and identifying which light is which at night.

### Open-Hinge Ergonomics

The Model 2343 "Open-Bridge" design allows fingers to wrap fully around the barrels for secure one-handed grip. The predecessor Navigator Pro (7155) uses a solid center bridge — less useful when one hand is on the helm.

## Warranty

**Steiner Heritage Warranty**: Lifetime repair or replacement, fully transferable, no registration card required. Valid on secondary/used market purchases. Electronics (compass illumination circuit) are covered separately for 3 years.

This makes used Steiner Navigator purchases low-risk — the optical and mechanical warranty follows the binoculars, not the original buyer.

## Singlehanding Notes

- **Sports-Auto-Focus is not optional for singlehanding** — focus-chasing on a moving boat while alone is dangerous
- Compass-in-view enables one-handed bearing-taking while maintaining situational awareness
- 7.14mm exit pupil important for dawn/dusk departures and arrivals common in Maine
- NBR rubber armor provides grip even with wet or gloved hands
- Yellow Steiner float strap is available — use it; 39 oz of binoculars sinking is a bad day

### Singlehanding Implication

The individual eyepiece focus on the Bushnell is a meaningful disadvantage for singlehanding. It's sharper per-eye once set, but re-focusing when conditions change (haze, distance shift) requires two separate adjustments. More importantly, there is no equivalent to Steiner's Sports-Auto-Focus — you cannot pre-set it to cover the entire useful range (20m–∞) and simply raise the binoculars. On a moving boat, alone, with one hand on the tiller, focus fiddling is a real problem.

**Verdict:** The Bushnell is a solid choice if budget is the constraint and you accept manual focus. The floating feature is a meaningful advantage over Steiner — if lost overboard, a $306 loss is less catastrophic than $690. But if you can afford the Steiner, Sports-Auto-Focus is worth the premium for singlehanded work.

### Bushnell H2O 7x50 — Not Recommended

The Bushnell H2O 7x50 (~$96.14) is waterproof but **does not include a compass**. Do not buy this if navigation bearing capability is required.
