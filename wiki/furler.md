# Harken Furler — Model Identification and Parts

SeaNymph has a Harken furler. The specific model has not been confirmed from the mast — identifying the exact unit (Unit 0 MKIV, Unit 0 ESP, Unit 0 MKIV Ocean, or Unit 1 MKIV) is a prerequisite for ordering any replacement part. The wrong unit part will not fit.

---

## Why This Matters

Harken uses a hierarchical naming convention: **Unit size** (0, 1, 2...) refers to boat length range; **product line** (MKIV, MKIV Ocean, ESP, MKIII) refers to the specific engineering variant. Parts are compatible only within the same unit-size and product-line combination.

The CD25D is a 25-foot boat — it falls within both the Unit 0 range (20–30 ft) and the Unit 1 range (22–30 ft, depending on headstay length). **Measuring the headstay and inspecting the furler drum is the only way to confirm which unit is installed.**

---

## How to Identify the Furler

Look for an embossed or printed model designation on the furler drum body. Harken typically stamps the product line there. Also check:

- **Unit 0 headstay wire range:** 3/16", 5/32", 7/32" (4–6 mm)
- **Unit 1 headstay wire range:** 5/32" to 9/32" (4–7 mm)
- **Drum size:** Unit 0 drum is noticeably smaller than Unit 1

When in doubt, take a photo of the drum and compare to the Harken product catalog, or call Hamilton Marine Rockland (207-594-8181) with the photo.

**Purpose of the long link plate:** Elevates the furler drum above the bow anchor/roller, preventing interference. The long link plate raises the drum height compared to a standard toggle. If the bow anchor or roller is close to the drum, this is the part that creates clearance.

> **Note:** The 7311.21 series is for Unit 1 only. Do not order this part for a Unit 0 furler.

## Older Unit 0 (MKIII 00AL)

If the furler is older (pre-MKIV), look for a "MKIII" designation. The MKIII 00AL uses a different part series (HFG prefix), with different clevis pin sizes. A professional rigger should confirm compatibility before ordering any MKIII parts.

## Operational Rules

**Never use a powered winch on the furling drum.** The furler is designed to be rolled by hand pull on the furling line, or at most by a manual winch handle. A powered winch can destroy the foil joints and overtension the luff tape.

**Routine maintenance:**
- Rinse the drum and foil joints with fresh water after every saltwater use
- Lubricate the roller bearing with a Teflon or silicone lubricant annually (not WD-40 — it leaves residue)
- Inspect all cotter pins and split rings annually; replace any that are bent or show corrosion
- Inspect headstay wire for corrosion and hairline cracks near swaged terminals

**Headstay replacement interval:** Harken recommends replacing headstay wire used in saltwater after **4 seasons or 6 years**, whichever comes first. A failed headstay loses the mast.
