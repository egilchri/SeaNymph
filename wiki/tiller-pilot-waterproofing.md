# Tiller Pilot — Waterproofing and Protection

The Raymarine ST1000 and ST2000 are **not waterproof**. This is the most common cause of failure on these units. Protecting the pilot from water is an ongoing maintenance task, not a one-time modification.

## Failure Modes

### 1. Water Ingress (Most Common)

Water enters through:
- Button panel / control face
- Seam between the body and the ram housing
- Connector junction at the power/SeaTalk plug

Consequences: corrosion of internals, intermittent failure, complete failure. Units that live in the cockpit permanently without protection typically fail in 1–3 seasons in Maine conditions.

### 2. Broken Drive Belt

The ST2000 uses an internal drive belt to convert motor rotation to linear push/pull motion. The belt:
- Fatigues over time, especially with hard use in heavy seas
- Can snap suddenly, leaving the pilot unable to push/pull
- Is replaceable — Raymarine parts are available; some sailors stock a spare belt

**Singlehanding implication:** Belt failure at sea means hand-steering immediately. In good conditions this is manageable; in a steep chop it is exhausting quickly. Keep a spare belt aboard. Learn the replacement procedure before you need it.

## Protection Approaches

### Primary: Remove When Not in Use

The simplest and most effective approach. If the pilot is not actively in use:
- Disconnect it from the socket and the tiller pin
- Bring it below (nav station or cabin)
- This eliminates all exposure

For singlehanding, the pilot will be in use frequently — but there will be long stretches (daysailing, motoring in harbor, at anchor) when it can live below.

### Silicone Sealing

Apply marine-grade silicone (Dow Corning 795 or equivalent) to:
- Seams between the body halves
- Around the button panel perimeter
- Around the connector entry point

**Caveat:** Silicone sealing can trap water if it's applied incorrectly. Ensure any sealing is watertight, not just visually covered. Do not use silicone that blocks the seam vent if the manufacturer intended it to be open.

### Button Panel Protection

The buttons are the main water entry point. Options:
- Thin silicone button covers (marine electronics dealers stock these)
- Stretch self-amalgamating tape over the panel
- Custom-sewn small oilskin flap (snap or velcro)

Applying a thin layer of silicone grease (not silicone sealant) to the button seals slows water intrusion.

### Saran Wrap / Plastic Wrap

A practical field solution: wrap the body of the pilot in Saran Wrap or clear plastic film, leaving only the controls accessible. Not elegant, but sailors report it works. Use rubber bands or electrical tape to secure. Replace when it degrades.

### Oilskin Sleeve Cover

A fitted oilskin (waxed canvas or technical fabric) sleeve that slides over the pilot when deployed:
- Can be sewn at home from oilskin fabric
- Leave the tiller end and the socket end open
- Should be removable without disconnecting the pilot (for adjustments)
- Multiple CD25D sailors have made these; dimensions vary by how the pilot is mounted

This is the most elegant solution for permanent cockpit deployment. Worth making before any extended passage.

### Commercial Covers

No manufacturer makes a purpose-designed waterproof cover for the ST2000 as of this research (2025). Third-party covers from marine accessory makers exist but fit varies. Check Defender and West Marine for current options.

## Storage Practice

When storing below:
- Dry the unit before stowing
- Store the drive ram (the extending arm) clean and lightly lubricated (light machine oil)
- Keep the connector covered with a dust cap or Ziploc
- Do not stow wet — internal corrosion will follow

End-of-season: dry thoroughly, inspect seals, apply light lubrication to the ram, bag and store below.

## Belt Replacement

The drive belt is a standard part. Steps (general — consult Raymarine service documentation):
1. Remove end caps (usually 4 screws)
2. Expose the drive mechanism
3. Remove the old belt from the motor pulley and the drive shaft
4. Route new belt and reinstall
5. Test stroke through full range before re-deploying

Having the Raymarine service manual (available online) is essential before attempting this. Stocking one spare belt is good practice.
