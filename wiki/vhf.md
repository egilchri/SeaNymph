# VHF Radio — DSC, MMSI, and Maine Communications

VHF marine radio is the primary emergency communications tool for Maine coastal sailing. Cell phone coverage is unreliable anywhere downeast or offshore. DSC capability on a modern VHF — with MMSI and GPS — is the closest thing to a near-shore EPIRB available on a small boat.

See also [[ais]] for AIS transponder (makes you visible to others), [[navigation-apps]] for chart and GPS integration.

---

## Cell Phone Limitations in Maine

Coverage is generally decent close to the coast but decreases significantly offshore or downeast. Signal boosters (e.g., waveform.com guide) can help but cannot penetrate a true dead zone. Do not depend on cell for emergency communications anywhere east of Penobscot Bay.

**USCG direct cell number:** `206-815-7220`  
Connects to USCG Sector Northern New England; select numeric options for Station Rockland. The 206 area code is correct — not a typo.

---

## Why VHF Beats Cell for Emergencies

Two decisive advantages of VHF channel 16 over calling the Coast Guard by phone:

1. **Every vessel within range hears your call** — nearby boats can often reach you faster than the Coast Guard.
2. **The Coast Guard gets a bearing on your position within 3 seconds** of your transmission via the Rescue 21 system. Still give your lat/lon explicitly for precise confirmation.

## DSC — Digital Selective Calling

DSC is the emergency and selective-calling feature built into modern VHF radios. It is only useful if:

- An **MMSI number** is programmed into the radio
- The radio is **connected to a GPS source** (or has internal GPS)

### Getting an MMSI Number

| Source | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **BoatUS** | $25 | boatus.com/products-and-services/membership/mmsi — simplest option |
| **FCC** | $100 | Required if sailing internationally; also assigns a Ship Station callsign |

Once obtained, MMSI is permanent and tied to the vessel — program it in and don't change it.

### Rescue 21 — Near-Shore Quasi-EPIRB

When you push and hold the **Distress button** on your DSC VHF:

- Broadcasts your position and MMSI to every vessel within range
- Signal is automatically relayed until it reaches a USCG shore station
- Distress signal continues broadcasting automatically until the USCG responds on channel 16
- Coast Guard can get an approximate bearing within 3 seconds

This makes DSC+GPS+MMSI a near-shore emergency beacon system — not a replacement for an EPIRB offshore, but highly effective within Rescue 21 coverage along the Maine coast.

### Group Call Function

DSC Group Call allows directed communication with a defined group of vessels — useful for club cruises or an informal group of cruisers traveling together.

### Reference

CCA member Charles Starke has written articles on DSC use, some freely available at: cruisingclub.org/communications/dsc-groups

## Practical VHF Habits

- **Emergency scripts:** Have a written MAYDAY/PAN PAN script accessible in the cockpit. Many modern chart plotters display emergency VHF scripts when you press the SOS button. As a singlehander, you will have to make the radio call alone, possibly while managing a developing situation — a posted script means you don't have to recall the format under stress.
- **Channel 16 always:** Monitor 16 underway. Switch to working channels (68, 69, 71, 72, 78A) for conversations; return to 16 when done.
- **MAYDAY relay:** If you hear a MAYDAY and the Coast Guard does not respond, you are obligated to relay it.

## Foghorn Activation by VHF

Foghorns within a half-mile of your position can be activated **on demand**:

> Switch to **channel 83A** and key your mic **five times** rapidly.

This is useful when entering a harbor entrance or transiting a buoy field in fog — you can confirm the location of a horn before you can see it.

## Starlink Mini — Satellite Internet Option

For downeast Maine or offshore passages where cell is unavailable, Starlink Mini provides high-speed internet:

| Spec | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Hardware** | ~1 ft square, 1 inch thick, built-in WiFi router |
| **Price** | $229–$350 (hardware) |
| **Power** | Requires 12V-to-30V converter for 12V house bank |
| **Plans (within 12 mi coast)** | Roam 50: $50/month; Roam Unlimited: $165/month |

Connect a phone or laptop to Starlink Mini's WiFi network. Does not replace VHF for safety communications — Starlink is for information/weather/messaging; VHF is for emergency.

## Singlehanding Notes

- The article notes "at least two people on board should know how to use the VHF." As a singlehander, you are the only one — know DSC cold, including how to activate the Distress button on your specific radio model. This is not something to work out while the boat is in trouble.
- Post a laminated MAYDAY script (position, nature of distress, number of persons aboard, vessel description) somewhere visible from the companionway.
- Program your MMSI and connect GPS **before the season, not when you need it.**
