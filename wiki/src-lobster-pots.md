# How to Deal with Lobster Pot Buoys — CCA / Ernie Godshalk

**Author:** Ernie Godshalk
**Origin:** Cruising Club of America (CCA) online cruising guide
**File:** raw/DealWithLobsterBuoys.md

## Summary

The most technically detailed article in the wiki on lobster pot navigation. Covers gear configuration in current (single and two-buoy), the current-direction avoidance rule, how to read buoy types, and a complete step-by-step extrication procedure with equipment recommendations. Includes a firsthand account of a Muscle Ridge Channel fouling that required a professional diver.

## Key Takeaways

- **Pass on the down-current side** of any buoy — the warp angles upstream and down; the up-current side is the danger zone
- **Stick buoy:** pass close to the stick side; **hardshell buoy:** pass on the rounded end
- **Two-buoy systems:** line between buoys sags below surface — NEVER go between them; pass downstream of the downstream buoy or well up-current of the up-current buoy
- **Buoy lying on its side** = primary buoy holding warp from bottom; toggle may be downstream, possibly submerged
- **Take engine out of gear immediately** if you cannot avoid a buoy
- **Do NOT engage the propeller** once fouled
- **Pull warp to cleat** to take tension off fouled section — often allows it to work free
- **Nearby lobstermen will often help** — they want to save the gear too
- **Swimming under the boat in Maine water (50–60°F) requires wetsuit, mask, flippers, tether, stopped engine** — cold incapacitation is real
- Shaft Shark (prop shaft cutter) is the best preventive tool
- Knife on stern pulpit + telescoping boat hook with knife taped on = minimum deck tools
- Fiskars P973 anvil ratchet pruner cuts heavy warp off the prop from deck level
- **Fog: harder; Dark: prohibitive** — don't motor through lobster grounds at night
- Morning at anchor: walk around and check under counter and rudder before departing
- Real story: Muscle Ridge Channel — invisible submerged buoy, prop seized, warp burned into cutlass bearing, required lobsterman diver ($100), who also checked zincs and thru-hulls

## Contradictions / Surprises

> The two-buoy system detail is the most important new content — the previous wiki described the toggle-and-main-float anatomy (from the Rubadeau article) but didn't explain that the submerged line *between* them is the fouling hazard, or give the explicit rule to never go between them. This article makes the mechanism and the avoidance rule explicit.

> The detail that a buoy lying on its side in strong current is the *primary* buoy (not the toggle) is counterintuitive — you'd expect the lighter toggle to lie down. The key is that the primary buoy is being pulled *down* by the warp weight; the toggle is downstream and may be submerged.

> No contradictions with existing wiki content.
