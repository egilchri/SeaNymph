# Cape Dory 25D — Overview

The Cape Dory 25D is a 25-foot fiberglass sloop designed by Carl Alberg and built by Cape Dory Yachts (Marion, MA). It is Edgar's vessel. This page is the canonical reference for boat-specific facts and should be updated as more is learned about the specific hull.

## Design and Hull

- **Designer:** Carl Alberg
- **Builder:** Cape Dory Yachts, Marion, Massachusetts
- **Production years:** approximately 1975–1988
- **Hull type:** Full keel, transom stern, fiberglass
- **Displacement:** heavy for its length (~5,000 lbs)
- **LOA:** 25 ft
- **LWL:** ~20 ft
- **Beam:** ~8 ft
- **Draft:** ~3.5 ft (full keel)

The "D" designation distinguishes the diesel-auxiliary variant from earlier outboard versions.

## Characteristics and Reputation

The CD 25D is regarded as a sea-kindly, forgiving boat. Key characteristics:

- **Motion:** Heavy displacement + full keel = slow, comfortable motion in a seaway. Hobby-horses less than fin-keel boats.
- **Tracking:** Full keel tracks well and holds course, which is beneficial for singlehanding (less constant helm correction needed).
- **Stability:** High initial and ultimate stability; not a tender boat.
- **Cockpit:** Small, protected cockpit — good for singlehanding and offshore use.
- **Simplicity:** Traditional design with minimal systems complexity.

> **Note:** Full-keel boats maneuver differently under power than fin-keel boats — slower to respond to rudder, wider turning radius. Important for docking and tight quarters.

## Singlehanding Implications

The CD 25D has several features that suit singlehanding:

- Good directional stability reduces need for constant steering
- Full keel and heavy displacement are forgiving if you make an error
- Simple systems mean fewer things to go wrong
- Small cockpit keeps the sailor closer to everything

Challenges:
- Heavier to handle (sails, anchor gear) than a lighter boat
- Under power, slow rudder response demands planning ahead in marinas
- Reefing and sail handling require working on deck alone — jacklines and tethering are essential

## Known Issues and Quirks

> **Note:** This section grows as sources are ingested and experience accumulates.

- Older boats may have deck-to-hull joint issues (common to the era)
- Check keel bolts periodically — full-keel boats are subject to keel-bolt corrosion over decades
- Original wiring on 40-year-old hulls is often undersized or degraded

## Research Resources

### Carl Alberg Archive — Architectural Plans

Carl Alberg's personal archive — sketches, blueprints, and models for all his designs including the Cape Dory line — was auctioned by Swann Galleries and is now believed to be held by the **Peabody Essex Museum, Phillips Library** in Rowley, MA (accessible from Salem, MA location).

- **Email:** research@pem.org
- **Phone:** 978-542-1553

If you ever need original lines drawings or construction offsets for the CD25D, this is the primary source. The Sea Sprite Association obtained line drawings for their boat from the PEM — the same approach should work for Cape Dory models.
