# Seacock Backing Plates

The backing plate is the structural foundation that distributes the load of a seacock across the hull. The original CD25D backing plates were marine plywood — after 40 years in a bilge, they are at the end of their service life. A spongy or soft pad is a safety emergency.

> **If you can push a corner of the backing pad with your finger and it feels spongy, this is a critical safety issue. Prioritize repair.**

## Material Choice

| Material | Verdict | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **G10/FR-4 fiberglass laminate** | **Gold standard — use this** | Completely waterproof, will never rot or delaminate, bonds extremely well with epoxy. Hard on tools but permanent. |
| Epoxy-encapsulated marine plywood | Acceptable compromise | Must be void-free marine ply (BS 1088), fully encapsulated in epoxy including over-drilled/re-drilled fastener holes. Vulnerable if encapsulation is ever breached. |
| StarBoard / HDPE polymer | **Do not use** | "Cold flows" under sustained compression — fasteners loosen over time. Does not bond with epoxy or sealants. Fundamentally unsafe for this application. |
| Pre-made Groco fiberglass discs | Good | Convenient alternative to cutting your own G10. Slightly more expensive. |

**G10 specs:** 1/2" to 5/8" thick. Size the plate to extend at least 1–2 inches beyond the seacock flange in all directions. Cut into circular, octagonal, or rounded-rectangle shape.

## Sealants

| Sealant | Use | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 3M 4200 / SikaFlex 291 | **Correct choice** for bedding through-hull and seacock | Strong, waterproof, removable in future |
| 3M 5200 | **Do not use** on through-hull threads or flanges | Permanent adhesive — makes future removal nearly impossible without destroying fittings |
| Thickened epoxy (colloidal silica) | Bonding backing plate to hull | Mix to peanut butter consistency; prevents sagging on curved hull |

## Installation Procedure

### Prep the hull surface — most critical step
1. Remove old pad completely (chisel, oscillating multi-tool)
2. Grind the interior hull with 36-grit disc — remove all old sealant, paint, gelcoat, expose raw rough fiberglass
3. Feather edges; wipe with acetone and let flash off completely
4. Tap finished area with screwdriver handle — sharp sound = solid. Dull thud = possible delamination (less likely on a CD25D).

### Bond the backing plate
1. Mix epoxy + colloidal silica to peanut butter consistency
2. Apply to both hull surface and back of G10 plate
3. Press firmly — epoxy should squeeze out around all edges (no voids)
4. Brace in place (stick braced across cabin, or through-hull loosely threaded through with plastic wrap)
5. **Cure 24 hours minimum before proceeding**

### Install through-hull and seacock
1. Drill center hole (correct hole saw size for new through-hull)
2. Dry-fit all hardware; confirm through-hull length, mark and cut to size
3. Drill mounting bolt holes using seacock flange as template
4. Countersink exterior mounting holes flush (for flat-head bolts)
5. Clean all surfaces with acetone
6. Apply 3M 4200 to: through-hull underside flange, threads, bolt heads, seacock flange base
7. Assemble: bolts through hull from outside → seacock over bolts inside → through-hull from outside
8. Tighten nuts evenly; sealant should squeeze out slightly at base
9. **Do not overtighten bronze bolts — they stretch**
10. Clean squeeze-out; fair exterior bolt heads with epoxy filler once cured
11. **Wait 48 hours minimum before launching**

### Post-launch inspection
Inspect immediately after launch. Check every 15–30 minutes for first few hours. Check again next day. Any weeping = haul out and investigate.

## Parts List (Per Seacock)

- 1× flanged all-bronze seacock (UL-Marine rated) — Groco FBV series or Apollo/Conbraco
- 1× bronze mushroom-head through-hull fitting (NPS straight threads)
- 3× silicon bronze machine screws (length: hull + backing plate + flange + nut)
- 3× silicon bronze nuts + washers
- 3M 4200 or SikaFlex 291 sealant
- G10 board (1/2" thick)
- West System epoxy + 406 colloidal silica

## DIY Cost Estimate (Maine, 2025)

**Per seacock, materials only:**
- Groco FBV-750 seacock: ~$60–95
- Groco TH-750 through-hull: ~$25–40
- G10 backing plate material: ~$20–40
- West System 105/205 kit: ~$55–70
- West System 406 colloidal silica: ~$20–25
- 3M 4200 sealant: ~$25–35
- Supplies (acetone, cups, etc.): ~$20–30
- **Total materials per seacock: ~$225–335**

Hamilton Marine (Searsport, ME) stocks all of these.

**Professional cost (New England yards, 2025):** ~$1,800–2,500 per seacock including haul-out.

## DIY Time Estimate

- Day 1 (demolition + prep + bond backing plate): 3.5–5.5 hours hands-on + 24 hours curing
- Day 2 (install hardware): 1–2 hours hands-on + 48 hours curing before launch
- **Total hands-on: 4.5–7.5 hours per seacock**
- **Total elapsed: minimum 2–3 days** (cure times are non-negotiable)

Doing all five seacocks in a batch is more efficient than doing them one at a time — one haul-out, shared materials, shared setup/cleanup time.
