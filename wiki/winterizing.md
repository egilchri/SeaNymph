# Winterizing the Cape Dory 25D

Annual winterization is the most consequential maintenance event of the year. Failures here — a cracked raw water pump, corroded batteries, mildew throughout the cabin — compound over time and are expensive to correct. Starting in 2025, marine insurers in Maine now treat professional winterization as a documentation requirement for claim defense; a DIY-only winterization with no written record is a liability in the event of a loss.

The four threats to address are: **freezing**, **corrosion**, **moisture and mildew**, and **system degradation**.

---

## Planning and Timing

Winterize after the last sail of the season, before hard frost threatens. In Penobscot Bay, that typically means October. Engine work is easiest while the engine is still warm from its last run.

**Professional winterization consideration:** Given the 2025 insurance landscape in coastal Maine, professional documentation of at least the cooling system antifreeze is worth having. If a freeze claim is ever disputed, a signed work order is the strongest defense. See [[insurance]].

## Yanmar 1GM Engine Winterization

### Oil Change

Change engine oil while the engine is warm — immediately after motoring in. Warm oil is less viscous and suspends combustion acids that would otherwise settle and corrode the engine over winter.

- **Oil type:** API CB or CC grade (per official Yanmar 1GM service manual — see [[engine]])
- **Viscosity:** SAE 20W or 30 for Maine coastal temperatures (10–25°C typical operating range)
- **Capacity:** 1.3 L crankcase + 0.25 L KM2A clutch case — **same oil, fill both**
- Change the oil filter at the same time

> **Note:** Prior wiki content incorrectly listed API CD and 15W-40 — corrected from the official service manual. Both crankcase and clutch case use the same oil.

### Cooling System Antifreeze — The Most Critical Step

The raw water cooling system must be flushed with antifreeze. This is the winterization step most likely to cause catastrophic damage if skipped or done wrong.

**Bucket method:**
1. Locate the seacock for the raw water intake (typically under the engine)
2. Fill a bucket with pre-mixed pink RV/marine antifreeze (not automotive) — at least 2 gallons
3. Place the bucket **inside the boat at pump height** — not on the dock or ground; the raw water pump cannot prime from below (14 L/min pump flow rate; self-priming distance is limited)
4. Disconnect the raw water intake hose from the seacock and place it in the bucket
5. Close the seacock
6. Run the engine until pink antifreeze appears from the exhaust — this confirms the entire cooling circuit has been flushed

**Alternative:** For continuous flushing, a garden hose with adapter can supply fresh water to the raw water inlet while running the engine on land, then finish with antifreeze.

**Thermostat reference:**
- Thermostat opens at 42°C
- Fully open at 52°C
- Engine should reach operating temperature before antifreeze pass

> **Warning:** After winterizing, tag the raw water intake seacock as CLOSED. It must be opened before next season's first start. Running the engine dry for even a short time destroys the raw water pump impeller.

### Cylinders — Fogging Oil

After the cooling system is done, fog the cylinders:
1. Remove the raw water intake hose from the bucket (or stop the hose supply)
2. With the engine still running (or restart it briefly), spray fogging oil into the air intake
3. The engine will smoke — that's correct; it's coating the cylinder walls
4. Shut down while still fogging to leave oil on the cylinder walls
5. Some owners remove the injector and squirt fogging oil directly — either method works

### Fuel System

- Fill the tank to **95% full** — leaves room for expansion but minimizes condensation
- Add **fuel stabilizer** (STA-BIL or equivalent) per manufacturer dosage
- Add **biocide** (Biobor JF or Star Tron) — diesel in humid environments grows microbial contamination that clogs filters
- **Run the engine for 15 minutes** after adding stabilizer/biocide to circulate treated fuel through the injector pump and lift pump
- Change the fuel filter at the end of season if hours warrant

## Fresh Water System

- Drain the fresh water tank completely
- Pump out all water from the lines using the hand pump or electric pump
- Pour RV/marine antifreeze (~1 quart) into the tank; pump it through the lines until pink antifreeze exits the faucet
- Leave antifreeze in the lines over winter

---

## Batteries

- Remove batteries from the boat and store in a cool, frost-free location (garage, basement)
- Charge to full before storage; check every 4–6 weeks over winter with a maintainer (Battery Tender or similar)
- Never let lead-acid batteries freeze — a discharged battery freezes at -10°C vs. a fully charged battery at -65°C
- See [[batteries]] and [[battery-management]]

---

## Interior — Moisture and Mildew

- Remove all wet or damp gear (sails, lines, cushions, clothing)
- Wipe interior surfaces dry; leave lockers and cabinets open for air circulation
- Place moisture absorbers (DampRid buckets) throughout the cabin
- Leave a companionway vent cracked or install a ventilator cowl to allow air circulation under the winter cover
- Run a small dehumidifier if shore power is available over winter

---

## Standing and Running Rigging

- Inspect for broken strands, cracked swage fittings, worn sheaves, chafe at spreader tips
- Rinse all blocks, clutches, and deck hardware with fresh water
- Apply a thin coat of Lanolin or Teflon spray to stainless turnbuckles and toggles
- Coil and bag running rigging; store below or ashore

---

## Sails

- Remove and wash sails with mild soap and water
- Inspect for chafe, UV damage, torn batten pockets, broken slides
- Store dry in breathable bags
- Never store sails wet — mildew forms rapidly

---

## Winterization Checklist

**Engine:**
- [ ] Oil change (warm engine, API CB/CC, SAE 20W or 30, new filter — change crankcase AND clutch case)
- [ ] Raw water cooling system flushed with antifreeze (pink appears at exhaust)
- [ ] Cylinders fogged
- [ ] Fuel treated (stabilizer + biocide) and engine run 15 min
- [ ] Fuel filter changed if needed
- [ ] Raw water seacock closed and tagged

**Mechanical:**
- [ ] Fresh water system drained and antifreeze run through
- [ ] Batteries removed and on maintainer
- [ ] Throttle/gear cables lubricated

**Interior:**
- [ ] Wet gear removed
- [ ] Moisture absorbers placed
- [ ] Lockers open, ventilation provided

**Above deck:**
- [ ] Sails off, washed, stored
- [ ] Running rigging rinsed and stored
- [ ] Rigging inspected
- [ ] Winter cover installed (see [[winter-cover]])

**Dinghy and outboard:**
- [ ] Yamaha F2.5 carb run dry (close fuel shutoff, let engine die)
- [ ] Outboard flushed with fresh water
- [ ] Outboard stored on its side (tiller-side down)
- [ ] Zodiac deflated, rinsed, dried
- [ ] 303 Protectant applied to Zodiac tubes
- [ ] Zodiac seams and valves inspected

See [[dinghy]] for full dinghy/outboard winterization procedure.
