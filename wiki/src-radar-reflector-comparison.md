## Summary

AI-generated comparison of passive and active radar reflectors for a 25-foot coastal sailboat. Covers four categories: octahedral folding, tri-lens, tube, and active RTE. Recommends the Davis Echomaster for coastal day sailing on a budget, and the Viking Tri-Lens for consistent heeled performance. Notes that active RTEs (EchoMax Active-X) provide the highest protection but require 12V power.

## Key Takeaways

- **Davis Echomaster 152** (octahedral): $120–$135, 12 m² peak RCS — but performance degrades significantly when heeled past 10–15°; source calls it "best bang for your buck" for coastal day sailing
- **Viking Tri-Lens** (lens array): $250–$300, ~2 m² steady — preferred for heeling vessels; consistent return while the boat is leaning
- **Mobri S-2 / Plastimo** (tube): $70–$110, 2 m² peak — minimal windage, easy to rig on shrouds; explicitly called out as "better than nothing but barely" and inadequate for SAR scenarios
- **EchoMax Active-X** (active RTE): $700–$900 — amplifies incoming radar signal, audible alarm when being painted; highest performance, requires power
- For Maine fog specifically, source recommends Viking Tri-Lens over Echomaster for consistency in heeled sailing

## Contradictions / Surprises

**Significant contradiction with existing wiki:** This source recommends the Davis Echomaster (octahedral) for coastal day sailing as "best bang for your buck." The existing [[radar]] page — based on independent QinetiQ testing and US Sailing data — strongly recommends **against** octahedral reflectors, noting that their RCS collapses to near 0 m² when the boat is heeled at 15–20°. That assessment is better supported by test data. **Do not treat the Echomaster recommendation as a reason to reconsider; the existing guidance stands.**

Viking Tri-Lens at ~2 m² steady RCS is below the ISO 8729-1 minimum (2.5 m²), which aligns with the existing wiki's assessment of tri-lens type as "minimum acceptable" — below ISO but far better than octahedral.
