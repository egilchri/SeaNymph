# Seacock and Through-Hull Removal Techniques

Removing old, seized through-hulls on a 40-year-old boat is often the hardest part of a seacock replacement project. A multi-tiered strategy — conventional first, then escalate — is the professional approach.

## The Three-Tier Strategy

### Tier 1: Conventional Removal (Try First)

Unscrew the seacock from the through-hull, then unscrew the through-hull from the hull.

- Soak threads with penetrating oil (PB Blaster) for several days before attempting
- Use a heat gun on the exterior mushroom head to soften old sealant
- One person outside holding the through-hull; one person inside turning the seacock with a large pipe wrench
- A step wrench or spud wrench engages the internal lugs of the through-hull to hold it from spinning

**Realistic expectation on a 40-year-old CD25D:** This may not work. Original Spartan Marine fittings are often heavily seized. Proceed to Tier 2 or 3 without excessive struggle.

### Tier 2: Alternative Tools (If Tier 1 Fails)

**Cutting the interior nut** (often the preferred professional alternative):
- Use an oscillating multi-tool with a carbide blade, or a Dremel with a reinforced cutting wheel
- Cut the interior nut from inside the boat — avoids all risk to exterior gelcoat
- Once the nut is cut off, the through-hull can be pushed out

**Internal pipe wrench / nipple extractor:**
- Grips the entire inner circumference of the through-hull (not just two small lugs)
- Cam action bites harder as torque increases — mechanically superior to a step wrench for removal
- Available at industrial tool suppliers (Superior Tool and similar); standard plumbing tool

### Tier 3: Destructive Removal (Standard Professional Method)

Grinding the exterior mushroom head and knocking the fitting inward is **what boatyards routinely do** — it is not a failure, it is efficiency.

**Angle grinder method:**
1. Protect surrounding hull with heavy-duty tape or thin metal shield
2. Grind the exterior flange where it meets the threaded barrel until severed or thin enough to snap
3. Use a block of wood as a "drift" and tap the fitting inward with a dead-blow hammer or mini-sledge
4. Do NOT hit fitting directly with steel hammer — use the wood drift to distribute impact

**Safety for grinding:**
- Face shield or safety glasses (flying sparks)
- Respirator (antifouling paint dust is toxic)
- Gloves
- Work in short bursts — heat buildup can damage fiberglass resin

**Other destructive methods:**
- Reciprocating saw: cut two slits 180° apart through the through-hull from inside, collapse inward with chisel
- Oscillating multi-tool: slower but more precise in tight quarters
- Hole saw: drill out entire fitting (useful if upsizing the hole for a larger replacement)

## Step Wrench — Honest Assessment

The marine step wrench (Buck Algonquin 3BTHW50150, ~$50–75; Groco THT-530, ~$150–185) is marketed for installation but used for removal. **Know its limitations before buying.**

**Compatibility risk with Spartan fittings:** At least one owner confirmed the Buck Algonquin step wrench does not fit original Spartan through-hulls — the cylinder is fractionally too large and the groove too narrow. This is a documented, specific risk for CD25D owners. Do not assume a step wrench will fit without testing.

**Mechanical limitation:** The wrench engages only two small internal cast lugs. Under the high torque needed to break 40-year-old sealant bonds, these lugs frequently shear off before the fitting moves. The step wrench is a best-case-scenario tool for removal.

**Better budget option:** A **radiator spud wrench** from Home Depot (~$25) is functionally identical to the marine step wrench and confirmed by experienced professionals to be "basically the same tool." Fit may be slightly sloppy but the low cost makes it a reasonable first attempt before escalating.

## DIY Wrench Option

**Modified socket method** (proven by forum users):
1. Find a large socket with OD just smaller than the through-hull inner diameter
2. Grind two slots into the rim with an angle grinder to match the through-hull's internal lug positions
3. Use with a ratchet, extension, and breaker bar for high-torque removal
- Cost: price of one sacrificial socket

## Key Tool Summary

| Tool | Cost | Best For | CD25D Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buck Algonquin step wrench | $50–75 | Installation; removal if fittings cooperate | May not fit Spartan originals |
