# SeaNymph Deviation Assessment — AI Analysis

**Origin:** AI analysis of SeaNymph's actual deviation table
**File:** raw/IsMyDeviationTooMuch.md

## Summary

Direct verdict on whether SeaNymph's measured compass deviation is acceptable. Conclusion: no — the 9° maximum and 8 of 12 headings exceeding 5° meets the professional threshold for mandatory physical compensation. The cause is identified as the Yanmar diesel's "hard iron" magnetism in the cockpit area.

## Key Takeaways

- **ISO 25862:2019 standard:** residual deviation ≤ 4° — SeaNymph exceeds this on 8/12 headings
- **5° = mandatory adjustment trigger** in many jurisdictions
- **7–8° = professional threshold** where compensation is required — SeaNymph has multiple headings at 7–8° and a peak of 9°
- **9° error = ~810 feet off course per mile** — dangerous in channels and shoal water
- **Root cause:** Yanmar diesel hard iron magnetism near the cockpit compass mount
- **Interference check:** confirm no phones, handheld VHF, or steel objects were near compass when table was taken — any of those can add 7–8° themselves
- **Table is still usable** with corrections applied, but no margin for approximation
