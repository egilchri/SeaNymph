## Key Takeaways

- Tow non-floating painter at 3–5 ft from transom — keeps line taut and above water, eliminating prop-fouling risk
- Remove motor and all gear from Zodiac before towing; bridle tow line to both CD25D stern cleats
- Go 2–3 knots; make wide turns; bring dinghy alongside for final approach
- 2.5–3.5 HP 4-stroke gas motors are the right power range for the 8 ft Zodiac at displacement speeds; Suzuki DF2.5 is lightest at 30 lbs
- Electric motors cost 1.6–2.5× more upfront than equivalent gas; "fuel" cost is negligible once purchased
- **Temo 450 is the best electric option for this use case**: 11 lbs, integrated battery, floats, IP67, 40 min full/3 hr quarter throttle, charges from 12V at 8.3A (30 Ah for full charge), $1,699–1,999
- Temo 12V charger converter draws 30 Ah from SeaNymph's battery bank — significant but recoverable with solar in a day at anchor
- Temo limitation: reverse is weak (more brake than true reverse); not suited to tight harbor maneuvering
- Temo quirk: "farting noise" in swelly conditions reported by multiple reviewers — not a failure, just an odd characteristic
- Torqeedo Travel 1103 CS offers more power and range but costs $2,400–3,000 and weighs 38 lbs — overkill for an 8-foot Zodiac and harder to handle solo
- **Rockland-area Temo service contacts:** Ocean Pursuits boatyard, Midcoast Marine Electronics; TEMO US support: +1 (207) 200-5649
