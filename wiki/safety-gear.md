# Safety Gear — Required and Recommended

Safety gear for SeaNymph. Divided into USCG-required items (must be aboard to be legal), strongly recommended items (not required but important for Penobscot Bay coastal sailing), and singlehanding-specific gear.

---

## USCG Required — Vessel Under 26 Feet

### Personal Flotation Devices

One USCG-approved Type I, II, III, or V PFD for each person aboard. For SeaNymph (solo), minimum one.

**Recommended:** Combination **inflatable PFD/harness** (Type V with Type I or II performance when inflated) — this fills two requirements in one piece of gear. Look for:
- Automatic + manual inflation (both)
- Crotch strap — prevents the harness riding up and coming off in the water
- D-ring on front for tether attachment
- 150N or 275N buoyancy — 275N (offshore standard) is heavier but keeps an unconscious person face-up

Good options: Spinlock Deckvest 6D, Mustang Survival MD3183, Harken/Sospenders.

> **Singlehanding note:** A PFD in a locker is useless. The inflatable harness must be **worn** any time you are on deck offshore, in deteriorating conditions, or at night. Not clipped to you — on your body.

### Throwable Device

One Type IV throwable (ring buoy or horseshoe buoy). Must be immediately accessible — not in a locker.

**Recommended addition:** Attach a floating throw line (50+ ft) and a light stick for night use.

### Visual Distress Signals (VDS)

USCG requires visual distress signals for operation on coastal waters. Minimum:
- **Day:** 3 orange smoke signals or electronic VDS device
- **Night:** 3 parachute flares or electronic VDS device

**Typical kit:** 3 combination day/night parachute flares (counts as both day and night) — one kit covers the requirement.

> **Important:** Flares expire 42 months from date of manufacture (printed on the case). Inspect and replace before each season. Expired flares may not work; carry them as backup but don't count on them.

**Electronic alternative:** ACR ResQLink or similar PLB (see below) covers the VDS requirement in some interpretations; check with your insurer.

### Fire Extinguisher

Minimum one B-I (Type B, Class I) marine fire extinguisher. Required for any enclosed compartment, engine, or cooking fuel aboard.

- Check gauge at commissioning each season — green zone required
- Hydrostatic test every 6 years (stamped on cylinder)
- Replace at 12 years regardless of condition

**Best practice:** Two extinguishers — one accessible from cockpit (companionway), one in the cabin near the galley.

### Sound-Producing Device

USCG requires a means to signal in fog. For vessels under 65 feet, a horn or whistle is sufficient.

**Minimum:** Canister airhorn in the cockpit.

**Foghorn activation by VHF:** Channel 83A, key mic five times — activates nearby foghorns on demand. See [[vhf]].

### Navigation Lights

Required for operation between sunset and sunrise or in reduced visibility. The CD25D's factory lighting configuration should cover this. Verify all lights work at spring commissioning.

## Strongly Recommended

### Harness and Tether

A separate topic from the PFD/harness combo. You need a **tether** — a webbing strap with a clip at each end — to keep you attached to the boat.

- **Length:** 6-foot tether for working on deck (shorter keeps you closer to the boat); 8-foot for moving between cockpit and foredeck
- **Double-ended:** A tether with two clip lengths lets you attach the short end while moving and transfer to the long end when stationary
- **Load-rated:** Tether should be rated to at least 2,200 lbs breaking strength (ISO standard)

**Jacklines** run from bow to stern on each side — the backbone of the tether system. Clip in and then move; never unclip until re-attached. Materials:
- Flat webbing (1-inch) preferred — round line rolls underfoot
- Stainless wire is strong but can break without warning under shock load
- Route jacklines **inboard of the rail** — a fall should pull you onto the deck, not over the side

### EPIRB / PLB

A **PLB (Personal Locator Beacon)** or **EPIRB (Emergency Position Indicating Radio Beacon)** sends a 406 MHz distress signal via satellite to the USCG. Unlike VHF DSC, it works well offshore and in areas with no cell or VHF coverage.

| Device | Coverage | Registration | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PLB** | Worldwide (satellite) | Free with NOAA | Worn on body; activates manually; no subscription fee; ~12-hr battery |
| **EPIRB (Category II)** | Worldwide (satellite) | Free with NOAA | Mounted on boat; manual activation; larger battery life |
| **EPIRB (Category I)** | Worldwide (satellite) | Free with NOAA | Auto-activates when submerged; most reliable if boat sinks |

For Penobscot Bay coastal sailing, a **PLB worn on the harness** is the most practical option — it goes with you even if you go overboard. DSC VHF is also effective within Rescue 21 coverage (see [[vhf]]).

Register with NOAA for free at beaconregistration.noaa.gov — registration is required for the beacon to be useful.

### First Aid Kit

Offshore-capable first aid kit. Minimum contents for coastal sailing:
- Pressure bandages and wound closure strips
- SAM splint
- Elastic bandages
- Non-prescription pain relievers and antihistamine
- Seasickness medication (Meclizine or Scopolamine patches — prescription; have aboard before you need it)
- Space blanket (hypothermia risk is real in Maine waters)
- Gloves and CPR face shield

A wilderness first aid course (WFA) is valuable preparation for singlehanding.

### Bilge Pump — Manual

The CD25D has a bilge pump. Confirm it is functional and the handle is accessible from the cockpit. An electric bilge pump is a convenience; the manual pump is the backup when the battery is dead or the wiring has failed.

## Singlehanding-Specific Notes

- **PFD worn, not stowed** — as a singlehander, you have no one to throw you a lifering. The PFD must be on your body.
- **Harness clipped before deck work** — see [[singlehanding-overview]]
- **PLB or DSC within reach at all times** — ideally on your harness. If you go in the water, you need to be able to activate a beacon.
- **MAYDAY script posted** — write out your vessel name, MMSI, position source, nature of distress, number of persons aboard on a laminated card and mount it near the VHF. Under stress, you will forget the format. See [[vhf]].

## Annual Checklist

- [ ] Flare expiration dates checked; replace any expired
- [ ] PFD bladder inflated and holds pressure; re-arm kit inspected
- [ ] Harness and tether webbing inspected for UV degradation or chafe
- [ ] Fire extinguisher gauge in green zone; hydrostatic date current
- [ ] PLB/EPIRB registration current (free annual renewal at NOAA)
- [ ] Navigation lights operational
- [ ] Bilge pump functional
- [ ] First aid kit stocked and medications not expired
